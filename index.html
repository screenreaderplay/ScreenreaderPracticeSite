Screen reader practice: FlyAble Flights

For the Skyscanner screen reader course. FlyAble Flights is a made-up airline, built twice: once so it works well with a screen reader, and once with hidden problems. The two versions look the same.

Choose a version

  1. FlyAble Flights: accessible version
  2. FlyAble Flights: inaccessible version

Use Safari with VoiceOver. Keep these steps open on your phone, or in a second Safari window, while you use the practice site on your Mac.

Keys you'll need

VO means holding down Control and Option together.

Mac practice task 1 of 5: Get comfortable turning VoiceOver on and off (2 minutes)

  1. Turn VoiceOver on with your shortcut.
  2. Turn it off again.
  3. Repeat until it feels natural.

Mac practice task 2 of 5: Explore the page (5 minutes)

  1. With VoiceOver off, open FlyAble Flights: accessible version.
  2. Turn VoiceOver on. Move forward through the page with VO+Right arrow, and back with VO+Left arrow. Press Control to pause the speech whenever it gets too much.
  3. Find the cookie message and accept all cookies.
  4. Find the question "How much luggage is included?". Open it and read the answer.

Think about:

  • Can you reach and understand all the content?
  • Does the reading order make sense?

Mac practice task 3 of 5: Plan your trip (8 minutes)

On the accessible version, search for a return flight from Camelot to Avalon for 2 adults.

Where you're going

  1. Set Trip type to Return.
  2. In the From field, type "Cam" and choose Camelot from the suggestions.
  3. In the To field, type "Ava" and choose Avalon from the suggestions.

Think about:

  • Are you told how many suggestions there are?
  • Can you move into the list and choose one?

Your travel dates

  1. Before choosing any dates, press Search flights.
  2. Open Travel dates. Choose a departure date, then a return date.

Think about:

  • When you searched without dates, was the error announced?
  • Can you move around the calendar and choose dates?
  • When the calendar closes, where is your focus?

Passengers and cabin

  1. Open Passengers and cabin, and set Adults to 2.
  2. Change the cabin class.
  3. Close Passengers and cabin, and press Search flights.

Think about:

  • Is the updated number of adults announced?
  • Can you choose a cabin class?

Mac practice task 4 of 5: Explore and sort results (8 minutes)

Move by headings

  1. On the search results, press VO+U, then Left or Right arrow until you reach Headings.
  2. Arrow down to the heading that starts "Search results" and press Enter.
  3. Press VO+Command+H to jump from result to result, to the 3rd result.

Think about:

  • Do the headings help you understand and move through the results?

Save a flight

  1. Go back to moving item by item: press VO+Right arrow until you reach the 3rd result's Save button, then press VO+Space.

Think about:

  • Are you told the flight is saved?

Sort the results

  1. Find the Best, Cheapest and Fastest tabs, and choose Cheapest.
  2. Move to the first result and listen to its price, times and flight time.

Think about:

  • Are the sort options clear?
  • Are you told how many flights there are?
  • Are the flight details easy to follow?

Mac practice task 5 of 5: Find the accessibility problems (10 minutes)

  1. Open FlyAble Flights: inaccessible version.
  2. Search for a flight, filter the results, and save a flight.
  3. Use the testing checklist below to find as many problems as you can.

Don't get stuck on one problem. Note it, move on, and we'll talk about what you found together.

Testing checklist

  • Navigating: can you reach everything on the page, and move around easily?
  • Understanding: are labels, instructions and information clear, with any hints you need?
  • Operating: can you use all the controls and finish your task?
  • Managing focus: does your focus move where you expect, and stay there?
  • State and feedback: are selections, changes and updates announced?

In more detail:

  • Are buttons and links descriptive? For example, "Close cookie message" rather than just "Close".
  • Can you use buttons, drop-downs and other controls?
  • When typing in a field with suggestions, are you told how many there are, and can you move into that list to choose one?
  • If something is shown by colour, such as a low price, is it also announced?
  • In the calendar, after picking your departure date, are you still in the calendar to pick your return date? Does the calendar then close and put you back where you started?
  • Are headings being announced as headings? Something can look like a heading but not be announced by the screen reader if it isn't marked up as one.
  • Can you move through the search results using headings?
  • Are updates announced, for example when you add a passenger, or when there is an error?

Mobile practice task 5 of 5: Test a Skyscanner page (10 minutes)

  1. Pick any Skyscanner page or journey you're working on, or would like to test, and open it in your phone's browser.
  2. Use the testing checklist below to see how well it works with your screen reader.
  3. Turn your screen reader off and post what you found in the Zoom chat.

Don't get stuck on one problem. Note it, move on, and we'll talk about what you found together.

Testing checklist

  • Navigating: can you reach everything on the page, and move around easily?
  • Understanding: are labels, instructions and information clear, with any hints you need?
  • Operating: can you use all the controls and finish your task?
  • Managing focus: does your focus move where you expect, and stay there?
  • State and feedback: are selections, changes and updates announced?

More to try on both versions, on your Mac or your phone, during the session or afterwards.

  1. Open the cookie settings, turn on Analytics cookies only, and save.
  2. Move the Popular destinations slideshow to Camelot and explore it. Then activate "Search flights to Camelot". What happens?
  3. What is the policy on bringing a pet dragon in the cabin?
  4. Open the calendar from Travel dates. Go to next month, use the Cheapest button to find the cheapest day, then choose it. Then, still in the calendar, choose a return date a week later. When the calendar closes, where are you, and do you know what to do next?
  5. Find out what "Include nearby airports" means, using the help button next to it.
  6. Turn on Direct flights only and Flexible dates, then search.
  7. Filter to flights that depart before 12:00. How do you know the list changed?
  8. Select a flight. Leave the email empty and try to confirm. Then fix it, add a checked bag, accept the terms and confirm.
  9. Find your booking reference, close the booking and check where your focus lands.

Grouped under the five areas of the testing checklist.

Navigating

  • From and To: typing announces how many results there are, but the results are hidden from screen readers. Down arrow does nothing, and swiping or exploring by touch can't reach them. WCAG 4.1.2
  • Search results: each flight's price and departure time look like a heading but aren't marked up as one, so you can't jump from flight to flight by heading. WCAG 1.3.1
  • The cookie message shows at the bottom of the screen straight away, but it is last in the page, so screen reader users don't hear about it until the end. WCAG 1.3.2, 2.4.3

Understanding

  • From and To: the instructions say to press Down arrow to reach the results, which makes no sense on a phone. WCAG 3.3.2
  • Calendar days only say the number and price, for example "30 £106". The month, weekday, low or high price and "select as departure date" are missing. WCAG 1.3.1, 2.4.6
  • The calendar's heading just says "Select dates" and never changes, so nothing tells you whether you are choosing your departure or your return date. WCAG 2.4.6, 3.3.2
  • The cookie buttons just say "Accept", "Close" and "Settings", and after choosing, "Change" and "Close". "Close" actually rejects optional cookies, which nothing tells you. WCAG 2.4.6, 3.3.2
  • Popular destinations: images have no alt text, so file names may be read. The Previous and Next buttons have no names, so they are read as symbols, and slides are labelled only "Slide 1", "Slide 2" and so on. WCAG 1.1.1, 4.1.2
  • The buttons that close Passengers and cabin and the booking confirmation both just say "Done", so out of context you can't tell what they close. WCAG 2.4.6
  • Search results: every result's buttons are called just "Save" and "Select", so you can't tell which flight they belong to. WCAG 2.4.6

Operating

  • The swap button between From and To only works with a mouse: it has no name and no role, and you can't reach it with the keyboard. WCAG 2.1.1, 4.1.2
  • From and To: the results can only be chosen with a mouse. WCAG 2.1.1
  • Cabin class is a home-made drop-down that only works with a mouse. You can't reach it with Tab, it isn't announced as a drop-down or button, and the "Cabin class" label isn't linked to it. WCAG 2.1.1, 4.1.2, 1.3.1

Managing focus

  • Calendar: it opens, but focus stays on Travel dates. It is not modal, so you have to go looking for it. WCAG 2.4.3
  • After you choose a departure date, focus is thrown to the top of the page, while the calendar stays open waiting for a return date. WCAG 2.4.3, 3.2.2

State and feedback

  • Main navigation: "Flights" is underlined as the current page, but screen readers aren't told it is the current page. WCAG 1.3.1
  • Trip type: Return and One way are radio buttons, but neither is ever announced as selected. WCAG 4.1.2
  • Calendar: nothing tells you it has opened. WCAG 4.1.3
  • Calendar: low and high prices are shown only by colour. WCAG 1.4.1
  • When you search, a "Searching for flights…" spinner appears, but nothing tells a screen reader user that the search has started. WCAG 4.1.3
  • Search errors, such as "Choose your travel dates", appear in red on screen, but nothing is announced: focus stays on the Search button and the fields aren't marked as having an error. WCAG 3.3.1, 4.1.3
  • The Save button never says whether the flight is saved. WCAG 4.1.2
  • Changing the sort or the filters updates the results silently, so you aren't told the list has changed or how many flights it now shows. WCAG 4.1.3

FlyAble Flights is fictional. Flights, airlines and places are invented for screen reader practice.
