FlyAble Flights practice site

A pretend flight booking site for practising with a screen reader. The same page is built twice: once accessibly, and once with hidden faults.

Mac practice tasks, with VoiceOver

Use Safari. Headphones help, so you can hear VoiceOver clearly. Press Control at any time to stop VoiceOver talking.

Keys you'll need: VO means holding down Control and Option together.

  • Turn VoiceOver on or off: Command+F5 (on some keyboards, Fn+Command+F5), or press Touch ID three times quickly
  • Move forward or back: VO+Right arrow or VO+Left arrow
  • Activate a link or button: VO+Space
  • In an open list: Up and Down arrows to move, Enter to choose
  • List headings: VO+U, then Left or Right arrow until you reach Headings
  • Next or previous heading: VO+Command+H or VO+Command+Shift+H

Mac practice task 1 of 5: Turn VoiceOver on and off (2 minutes)

  1. Turn VoiceOver on with your shortcut, then turn it off again.
  2. Repeat until you're comfortable doing it without thinking.

Mac practice task 2 of 5: Can you reach all the content on the page, and does it make sense? (5 minutes)

  1. With VoiceOver off, open the Accessible version.
  2. Turn VoiceOver on with your shortcut.
  3. While VoiceOver is reading, press Control to pause it. Get used to doing this whenever the speech gets too much.
  4. Find the cookie message. Open the cookie settings, turn on Analytics cookies only, and save.
  5. What is the policy on bringing your pet dragon on a flight?
  6. Find out whether there is step-free access at Mount Olympus airport.
  7. In Popular destinations, move the slideshow to the Camelot slide and explore its content.
  8. Press the "Search flights to Camelot" button. What happens?
  9. Turn VoiceOver off and post "done" in the Zoom chat.

Mac practice task 3 of 5: Can you use every part of the form, and does focus go to the right place? (10 minutes)

  1. Book a return flight from Camelot to Avalon. Search by typing part of each place name, then choosing from the results.
  2. Choose 1 adult and 2 children.
  3. Choose Business class.
  4. Don't pick any dates yet.
  5. Press Search flights. What happens? Was the error announced?
  6. Now add your dates and press Search flights again.
  7. Stop when you reach the search results.

Mac practice task 4 of 5: Can you understand the page and move through the results using headings? (5 minutes)

  1. On the search results, press VO+U and go to Headings. Do the headings make sense?
  2. Arrow down to the heading that starts "Search results" and press Enter.
  3. Press VO+Command+H to jump forward through the results. Go to the 3rd result.
  4. Find that result's Save button and select it. Were you told it was saved?

Mac practice task 5 of 5: Accessibility testing (10 minutes)

  1. Open the Inaccessible version.
  2. Use your new VoiceOver skills to find as many accessibility problems as you can, using the checklist below.

Testing checklist

  • Navigating: can you move to everything on the page, and is it easy to do so?
  • Understanding: is everything read out, with any extra hints you need? Are changes to the page announced?
  • Operating: can you use all the controls on the page?
  • Managing focus: when you finish something, does your focus go to the right place?
  • State: are things like selected or not selected, and expanded or collapsed, announced?

In more detail:

  • Are buttons and links descriptive? For example, "Close cookie message" rather than just "Close".
  • Can you use buttons, drop-downs and other controls?
  • When typing in a field with suggestions, are you told how many there are, and can you move into that list to choose one?
  • If something is shown by colour, such as a low price, is it also announced?
  • In the calendar, after picking your departure date, are you still in the calendar to pick your return date? Does the calendar then close and put you back where you started?
  • Are headings marked up as headings? Something can look like a heading but be ignored by the screen reader if it isn't marked up as one.
  • Can you move through the search results using headings?
  • Are updates announced, for example when you add a passenger, or when there is an error?

Phone practice tasks, with VoiceOver or TalkBack

Use Safari on iPhone with VoiceOver, or Chrome on Android with TalkBack. You'll use the same practice site, in your phone's web browser.

Gestures you'll need:

  • Turn the screen reader on or off: iPhone, press the side button three times (the Home button on older iPhones). Android, press and hold both volume keys for a few seconds.
  • Move forward or back: swipe right or swipe left with one finger
  • Activate the item you're on: double tap anywhere on the screen
  • Pause or carry on speaking: tap with two fingers
  • Hear what's under your finger: drag one finger over the screen
  • Move by headings on iPhone: twist two fingers on the screen, like turning a dial, until you hear "Headings". Then swipe down for the next heading and up for the previous one.
  • Move by headings on Android: swipe up or down with three fingers until you hear "Headings". Then swipe down with one finger for the next heading, and up for the previous one.
  • Typing on iPhone: switch Typing Style to Touch Typing (Settings, Accessibility, VoiceOver, Typing), so you just lift your finger to type a letter.

Phone practice task 1 of 5: Turn your screen reader on and off (3 minutes)

  1. Turn your screen reader on with your shortcut, then turn it off again.
  2. Repeat until you're comfortable doing it without thinking.

Phone practice task 2 of 5: Can you reach all the content on the page, and does it make sense? (5 minutes)

  1. With the screen reader off, open the Accessible version in your phone's browser.
  2. Turn your screen reader on with your shortcut, and swipe right through the page.
  3. While it is reading, tap the screen with two fingers to pause the speech, and tap with two fingers again to carry on. This works on iPhone and Android.
  4. Find the cookie message. Open the cookie settings, turn on Analytics cookies only, and save.
  5. How much luggage is included in every fare?
  6. Find out how much it costs to change your dates after booking.
  7. In Popular destinations, move the slideshow to the Camelot slide and explore its content.
  8. Double tap the "Search flights to Camelot" button. What happens?
  9. Turn your screen reader off and post "done" in the Zoom chat.

Phone practice task 3 of 5: Can you use every part of the form, and does focus go to the right place? (10 minutes)

  1. Book a return flight from Camelot to Avalon. Search by typing part of each place name, then swipe right to reach the results and double tap to choose one.
  2. Choose 1 adult and 2 children.
  3. Choose Business class.
  4. Don't pick any dates yet.
  5. Double tap Search flights. What happens? Was the error announced?
  6. Now add your dates and double tap Search flights again.
  7. Stop when you reach the search results.

Phone practice task 4 of 5: Can you understand the page and move through the results using headings? (5 minutes)

  1. On the search results, set your screen reader to move by headings, and swipe down through them. Do the headings make sense?
  2. Move by headings to the heading that starts "Search results".
  3. Keep swiping down to jump forward through the results. Go to the 3rd result.
  4. Swipe right to that result's Save button and double tap it. Were you told it was saved?

Phone practice task 5 of 5: Test your own page (10 minutes)

  1. Pick any Skyscanner page or journey you're working on, or would like to test, and open it in your phone's browser.
  2. Use the testing checklist above to see how well it works with your screen reader.
  3. Turn your screen reader off and post what you found in the Zoom chat.

Extra practice

More tasks to try on both versions.

  1. Open the calendar from Travel dates. Go to next month, use the Cheapest button to find the cheapest day, then choose it. Then, still in the calendar, choose a return date a week later. When the calendar closes, where are you, and do you know what to do next?
  2. Find out what "Include nearby airports" means, using the help button next to it.
  3. Turn on Direct flights only and Flexible dates, then search.
  4. Sort the results by Cheapest. How do you know the list changed?
  5. Filter to flights that depart before 12:00.
  6. Select a flight. Leave the email empty and try to confirm. Then fix it, add a checked bag, accept the terms and confirm.
  7. Find your booking reference, close the booking and check where your focus lands.

  • Main navigation: "Flights" is underlined as the current page, but aria-current is missing, so screen readers don't say it is the current page. WCAG 1.3.1
  • Trip type: Return and One way are radio buttons, but neither is ever announced as selected. 4.1.2
  • The swap button between From and To only works with a mouse: it has no name and no role, and you can't reach it with the keyboard. 2.1.1, 4.1.2
  • From and To: the instructions say to press Down arrow to reach the results, which makes no sense on a phone. Typing announces "2 results available. Press Down arrow to choose.", but the results are hidden from screen readers: Down arrow does nothing, swiping or exploring by touch can't reach them, and they can only be chosen with a mouse. 2.1.1, 4.1.2, 3.3.2
  • Calendar: it opens, but focus stays on Travel dates and nothing tells you it opened. It is not modal, so you have to go looking for it. 2.4.3
  • Calendar days only say the number and price, for example "30 £106". The month, weekday, low or high price and "select as departure date" are missing. Low and high prices are shown only by colour. 1.3.1, 1.4.1, 2.4.6
  • The calendar's heading just says "Select dates" and never changes, so nothing tells you whether you are choosing your departure or your return date. 2.4.6, 3.3.2
  • After you choose a departure date, focus is thrown to the top of the page with no message, while the calendar stays open waiting for a return date. 2.4.3, 3.2.2
  • The cookie message shows at the bottom of the screen straight away, but it is last in the page, so screen reader users don't hear about it until the end and have to go to the bottom to dismiss it. 1.3.2, 2.4.3
  • The cookie buttons just say "Accept", "Close" and "Settings", and after choosing, "Change" and "Close". "Close" actually rejects optional cookies, which nothing tells you. 2.4.6, 3.3.2
  • Search results: each flight's price and departure time look like a heading but aren't marked up as one, so you can't jump from flight to flight by heading. You have to read through every detail of each flight to reach the next. 1.3.1, 2.4.6
  • Cabin class is a home-made drop-down that only works with a mouse. You can't reach it with Tab, it isn't announced as a drop-down or button, it doesn't say whether it's open, and the "Cabin class" label isn't linked to it, so screen readers just read "Economy" as plain text. 2.1.1, 4.1.2, 1.3.1
  • When you search, a "Searching for flights…" spinner appears, but nothing tells a screen reader user that the search has started. 4.1.3
  • Search errors, such as "Choose your travel dates", appear in red on screen, but nothing is announced: focus stays on the Search button and the fields aren't marked as having an error. 3.3.1, 4.1.3
  • Search results: every result's buttons are called just "Save" and "Select", so in a list of buttons you can't tell which flight they belong to. The Save button never says whether the flight is saved. 2.4.6, 4.1.2
  • Changing the sort or the filters updates the results silently, so you aren't told the list has changed or how many flights it now shows. 4.1.3
  • The button that closes Passengers and cabin, and the one that closes the booking confirmation, both just say "Done", so out of context you can't tell what they finish or close. 2.4.6
  • Popular destinations: images have no alt text, so file names may be read. 1.1.1
  • Popular destinations: the Previous and Next buttons have no names, so they are read as symbols such as "‹" or "left-pointing angle quotation mark". Slides are labelled only "Slide 1", "Slide 2" and so on, which tells you nothing about the destination. 4.1.2, 1.3.1

FlyAble Flights is fictional. Flights, airlines and places are invented for screen reader practice.
